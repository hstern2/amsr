AMSR¶

Another Molecular String Representation.

AMSR is a molecular string representation designed to be fault-tolerant, concise, and conformationally aware:

  • every sequence of tokens generates a “reasonable” molecule (you may have different ideas about what constitutes a reasonable molecule than this string representation);

  • common functional groups and aromatic rings can be represented by single tokens; and

  • a 3-D conformer can be encoded by assigning a coarse-grained dihedral angle to each rotatable bond.

AMSR is inspired by

  • H. Hiz, “A Linearization of Chemical Graphs,” J. Chem. Doc. 4, 173-180 (1964)

  • SMILES

  • PATTY

  • DeepSMILES

  • SELFIES

Usage¶

AMSR strings can be decoded directly to RDKit molecules:

import amsr

caffeine = amsr.ToMol("CNcncc5cNcN6C.oC.o")

SMILES or RDKit molecules can be encoded in the other direction:

taxol_smi = "CC1=C2[C@@]([C@]([C@H]([C@@H]3[C@]4([C@H](OC4)C[C@@H]([C@]3(C(=O)[C@@H]2OC(=O)C)C)O)OC(=O)C)OC(=O)c5ccccc5)(C[C@@H]1OC(=O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](NC(=O)c6ccccc6)c7ccccc7)O)(C)C"

amsr.FromSmiles(taxol_smi)
# CccCC`C`C`C'C`OC4.CC'C'6coC`8[OAc].C.O....[OAc].O[Bz].CC`6OcoC`O.C'N[Bz].[Ph]....O.C.C

amsr.FromSmiles(taxol_smi, useGroups=False)
# CccCC`C`C`C'C`OC4.CC'C'6coC`8OcoC..C.O....OcoC..Ococccccc6......CC`6OcoC`O.C'Ncocccccc6......cccccc6.........O.C.C

Conformational representation¶

When FromMol receives an RDKit molecule with a 3-D conformer, AMSR records the torsion around each eligible rotatable bond. Each torsion is rounded to the nearest 30° and written as a two-character bond token:

For example, [Cl]C/>C[Br] specifies a -60° Cl-C-C-Br torsion:

dihedral = {}
mol = amsr.ToMol("[Cl]C/>C[Br]", dihedral=dihedral)
# dihedral == {(0, 1, 2, 3): -60}

mol_3d = amsr.GetConformer(mol, dihedral=dihedral)

The reference atoms for a torsion are the earliest neighboring atoms on either side of the bond as they appear in the string. Consecutive dihedral tokens have an implicit carbon between them, which keeps ring conformations compact; for example, a cyclohexane chair can be written C/><\/><\/><\6.

The same 0° and 180° tokens are used for double-bond stereochemistry: ^^ means cis or Z, and __ means trans or E. Whether a token describes a rotatable-bond torsion or double-bond stereochemistry follows from the bond order.

Conformer round trips¶

A conformer can be encoded, decoded, and reconstructed as follows:

from rdkit import Chem
import amsr

source = Chem.MolFromMolFile("molecule.sdf", removeHs=True)
encoded = amsr.FromMol(source)

dihedral = {}
decoded = amsr.ToMol(encoded, dihedral=dihedral)
reconstructed = amsr.GetConformer(decoded, dihedral=dihedral)

GetConformer obtains trial coordinates from distance geometry, fixes chirality, E/Z geometry, ring puckers, and acyclic dihedrals, then optimizes all atom positions subject to ideal bond lengths and angles, planarity, stereochemistry, and the encoded dihedrals. Candidates with serious nonbonded clashes are rejected. amsr.Roundtrip(source) provides the same workflow and also returns the symmetry-aware heavy-atom RMSD to the source conformer.

The 30° discretization is deliberately coarse-grained: it keeps the conformational alphabet small while retaining enough geometry to reconstruct the overall conformer.

Rocaglamide¶

Rocaglamide illustrates how the topology and absolute stereochemistry can remain fixed while the dihedral tokens distinguish conformers:

Conformer A:

CNC.__co\>C`__C`^\C'^^C'/^C`_/5O..__(6)O\>5@.__[OMe].__[OMe]O.>>(6)..__[OMe]..\_[Ph]

Conformer B:

CNC.__co<<C`\_C`^^C'^\C'/^C`_/5O.._/(6)O\_5@.^^[OMe].__[OMe]O./>(6)..^^[OMe]..<\[Ph]

Both strings decode to rocaglamide. Their different two-character bond tokens retain the two arrangements shown above.

Groups¶

Groups are token-level abbreviations. They make strings shorter without introducing a second decoding mechanism: each group is expanded to ordinary AMSR atom and bond tokens before the molecule is constructed. FromMol and FromSmiles use groups by default; pass useGroups=False to obtain the fully expanded string.

Aromatic-ring groups¶

Parenthesized tokens encode an entire monocyclic aromatic ring. The number is the ring size; letters a through f are positions in string traversal order; and the element after a position replaces the carbon at that position. For six-membered rings, N indicates one or more pyridine-like nitrogens. Unlike [Ph], which is a capped phenyl substituent, (6) leaves the ring’s remaining valences available as the string continues.

nccccc6

(6acN)

(5aNbN)

nNccc5

pyrazole

The position letters describe the AMSR traversal, not conventional ring numbering. All supported five- and six-membered aromatic patterns are generated by amsr/aromatics.py and are available through amsr.Groups().

Inspecting and extending groups¶

amsr.Groups() returns the live group dictionary. Its keys are group tokens, and each value is a list of one or more equivalent AMSR expansions. The decoder uses the first expansion; the encoder recognizes all of them.

groups = amsr.Groups()
groups["[Ph]"]
# ["cccccc6....."]

groups["[PEG2]"] = ["OCCOCC...."]
amsr.InitializeGroups()

amsr.FromSmiles("COCCOCC")
# "C[PEG2]"

Call InitializeGroups() after modifying the dictionary so the encoder and decoder rebuild their lookup structures.

Language reference¶

Atoms¶

Atoms are represented by their symbol enclosed in square brackets, as in SMILES. For a one-letter symbol, brackets may be omitted. Atoms are assumed to have a fixed valence that limits the number of covalently-bonded neighbors. If an atom makes fewer bonds than its valence, hydrogens are assumed.

AMSR

molecule

C

methane

O

water

[Cl]

hydrochloric acid

Chains¶

Each atom in a chain is bonded to the most recently added atom that can still make bonds, according to its valence. Hydrogens may be added explicitly like any other atom. In the example below, the fluorines are added to the second carbon; the chlorine is then added to the first carbon, since the second can no longer bond.

AMSR

molecule

CCFFF[Cl]

2-chloro-1,1,1-trifluoroethane

Branches¶

Branches are formed automatically when atoms can no longer make bonds. They can also be made by “capping” or “saturating” an atom with hydrogens, using a period .. Capping hydrogens are applied to the most recently added atom that can still make bonds. New atoms will then be bonded to those added earlier, forming a branch.

AMSR

molecule

CCC.C

isobutane

CC.CC.C.C

2,2-dimethylbutane

Rings¶

Rings are denoted by a single digit, or two or more digits enclosed in square brackets, giving the size of the ring. A new bond is formed between the two most recently added atoms that can make bonds and, when bonded, will form a ring of that size.

AMSR

molecule

CCO3

oxirane

CCCCCC6

cyclohexane

CCCCCCCCCCCC[12]

cyclododecane

When more than one ring of a given size can be formed, one or more @ signs immediately after the digit make ring-forming bonds with atoms appearing earlier in the string, rather than the most recent.

AMSR

molecule

ccOcc5cccc6

benzofuran

ccOcc5cccc6@

isobenzofuran

Double bonds (sp2 centers)¶

Atoms making a double bond are indicated by changing the symbol to lowercase (note that lowercase does not mean “aromatic”; merely, “atom having one fewer neighbor than its valence”). Double bonds are assigned by a matching algorithm. If a perfect matching cannot be found, for instance in the case of an odd number of contiguous lowercase symbols, a maximal matching is chosen, non-matched atoms remain singly bonded, and hydrogens are added.

AMSR

molecule

co

formaldehyde

cccccc6

benzene

cco

acetaldehyde (only one double bond added)

An oxygen with two neighbors or a nitrogen with three in an aromatic ring is still denoted by a capital, not a lowercase, symbol. Although sp2-hybridized, its coordination number is still equal to its valence.

AMSR

molecule

ccccO5

furan

ccccN5

pyrrole

Triple bonds (sp centers)¶

Atoms with two fewer neighbors than their valence are designated by a trailing colon : and can make triple bonds or more than one double bond.

AMSR

molecule

C:N:

hydrogen cyanide

oC:o

carbon dioxide

Hypervalent atoms¶

Atoms denoted by their symbol alone are assumed to have their lowest possible valence (for instance, two for sulfur). Higher valences are denoted by one or more exclamation points !.

AMSR

molecule

CSC

dimethyl sulfide

Cs!oC

dimethyl sulfoxide

S!!FFFFFF

sulfur hexafluoride

Formal charges, radical electrons, and isotopes¶

Positive and negative formal charges are designated by one or more + or - signs. Radical electrons are denoted by one or more asterisks *. An isotopic mass is denoted by a number prefix before the atomic symbol, in which case square brackets must be used even for a one-letter symbol.

AMSR

molecule

[Mg++];S!!:ooO-O-

magnesium sulfate

CC.C.CCCCC.C.N6O*

(2,2,6,6-Tetramethylpiperidin-1-yl)oxyl or TEMPO

[2H]O[2H]

heavy water

Tetrahedral stereochemistry¶

Tetrahedral stereochemistry is denoted by a single quote ' meaning “clockwise” or a backtick character ` meaning “counterclockwise.” The direction refers to the first three neighbors of a stereocenter as they appear in the string, with the last neighbor or implicit hydrogen in back.

AMSR

molecule

C`C.FO

(1S)-1-fluoroethanol

C'C.FO

(1R)-1-fluoroethanol

E/Z stereochemistry¶

As described above, double-bond stereochemistry is denoted by __ meaning “trans” or E, or ^^ meaning “cis” or Z. The reference neighboring atoms are those that appear earliest in the string.

AMSR

molecule

c[Br][Cl]__c[Cl]

(E)-1-bromo-1,2-dichloroethene

c[Br][Cl]^^c[Cl]

(Z)-1-bromo-1,2-dichloroethene

Multiple molecules¶

More than one molecule may be specified by separating them with ;.

AMSR

molecule

ocC.O(6)[COOH];CcoN(6)..O

aspirin and acetaminophen
